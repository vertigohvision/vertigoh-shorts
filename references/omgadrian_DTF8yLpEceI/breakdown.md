# Breakdown: Adrian Per, "Do you want to make better content this year?" (IG, Jan 2026)

267K likes · **5.7K comments** (high comment ratio) · 66s · 23.98fps · −14.2 LUFS.
A channel re-introduction / manifesto. This is the polished, scripted end of the range.

## Hook (0–3s)
- **The first word lands at 0.11s.** No dead air.
- **Line 1 is a direct question** to the viewer's own goal: "Do you want to make better content
  this year?"
- **Line 2 promises he can solve it:** "Well, I can fix that for you."
- **Line 3 raises the stakes:** "And the crazy part? I haven't even shown you my best work."
  That's three hooks in 9 seconds.
- **Visual:** a moody, art-directed set (red carpet, lamp, leather recliner) in a wide shot,
  then a push-in to a medium at 2.5s. A camera change instead of a cut keeps energy up in the
  first 3s.
- **The key word is giant text:** small serif italic lead-in ("Do you wanna make") over a huge
  condensed yellow word ("BETTER", "YEAR?", "FIX THAT"), with a blur-in animation.

## Structure
| Time | Beat |
|---|---|
| 0–11s | Question hook, promise, "best work yet" open loop |
| 11–28s | Credibility montage: 3 years of content, shown fast (camera, editing, consistency) |
| 28–37s | Turn: "So let's try this again… My name is Adrian." |
| 37–55s | The new promise: "raising the bar", "deeper stories", "not shortcuts" |
| 55–66s | CTA: his IG profile on screen with a mouse clicking toward **Follow**; "See you soon" |

## Retention devices
- **24 shots, average 2.75s, cuts 3.5 per 10s.**
  - Every list item gets its own visual: "camera work" (car shifter), "editing" (timeline
    screenshot), "consistency" (calendar wall).
  - Literal B-roll illustrates each word.
- **The location, wardrobe and look change constantly** (studio, desert car, moto,
  black-and-white, white void, sunset). It's visually rich without being chaotic.
- **Two caption layers:**
  - Spoken subtitles: tiny serif, white, low.
  - The single key word: giant yellow condensed type, usually behind or around his head.
  - The eye always has a "headline" to read.

## Captions
- **Subtitle layer:** small serif italic, white, about 1/3 from the bottom, 2–4 words.
- **Emphasis layer:**
  - One or two words per beat in a heavy condensed yellow sans (BETTER, FIX THAT, AGAIN,
    DEEPER STORIES, LESSONS, UP, SHORTCUTS, SOON).
  - Paired with a script-italic lead-in.
  - Animated with a blur/scale-in, sometimes placed behind the subject (depth layering).
- **Consistent brand colour: yellow.**

## Ending / CTA
He *shows* the follow action: a screen recording of his profile with the cursor hovering
Follow. The CTA is visual, not just spoken. "See you soon" over a silhouette against an
orange sun = a branded end card.

## Why it worked (seen vs guessed)
- **Seen:** instant speech, a question hook that turns into a promise, a visual for every
  phrase, a consistent type system, and a visual follow CTA.
- **Guessed:** the comments come from the direct "you" framing and the new-year timing. People
  reply with their own goals.

## Steal / avoid
1. **The first word in under 0.3s.** Open on the viewer's goal, phrased as a question, then
   promise the fix.
2. **Two-layer type system:** small subtitles plus ONE giant emphasis word per beat in a brand
   colour. For Kai: Montserrat for subtitles, and test a heavy condensed display face or
   Montserrat ExtraBold for emphasis.
3. **Show every noun.** When the script lists things, cut to a visual of each one.
4. **Make the follow CTA visual** (screen-record the profile and the Follow button) and tie it
   to a promise of what's coming.
5. **Camera moves or punch-ins can stand in for cuts** in a talking head.
6. **Avoid (for Kai):** this is a fully produced manifesto. Borrow the systems (type, hooks,
   visual-per-noun), not the production level, for everyday shorts.
