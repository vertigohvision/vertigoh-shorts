## Why it flopped
**Seen (measurable):**
1. **No concrete hook.** The first 5.7s are an abstract question; all of Kai's winners open on a
   concrete action or promise (countdown, "This is how I got to fly for Netflix").
2. **Off-niche first frame.** Robots + desk talking head; Kai's FPV/filmmaking skill shows once, at
   7.1s, for ~1s. His winners show the pilot or rig in frame 1.
3. **No BTS proof or payoff shot.** There's no "result" to wait for.
4. **Length 75.8s** with no tension device to justify it (the IG #1 earns 79s with a one-take).
5. **Audio −19.8 LUFS**, about 6 dB under platform norm and the winners (−8.2, −14.2).
6. **The title has no information or searchable keyword.**

**Guessed:**
- The algorithm likely tested it with Kai's FPV/BTS audience, who swiped in the first 2–3s because
  the opening didn't match what they follow him for.
- The "recap" framing (what *I* did) only works for creators the audience already has a personal bond
  with. Kai's audience follows the craft, not yet the person.
- Kai liked it because it's personal and meaningful *to him*. That's a valid long-form or community
  post, but as a cold-traffic Short it gives strangers no reason to stay.

## Steal / avoid
1. **Avoid opening on an abstract question.** If the theme is "will AI replace me", open on a concrete
   test ("I let a robot film my shot. Here's what happened").
2. **Avoid a first frame that doesn't show Kai's craft.** Pilot, rig or BTS proof in frame 1.
3. **Every recap needs one hero moment** (a shot or stunt) that's teased at 0s and paid off at the end.
4. **Title = topic + stake** ("Flying FPV with 23 creators in China"), not a mood.
5. **Loudness at −14 LUFS**: check every export.
