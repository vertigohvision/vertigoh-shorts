## What separates them (and what doesn't)

**Doesn't separate on its own:**
- **Speech by 0.3s.** 2 of the 3 flops speak at 0.00s (China, Avata). It's necessary, not sufficient.
- **Cut rate.** The two biggest winners have **0 cuts**; the China flop cuts 6.2 times per 10s. Pacing
  comes from *what changes*, not from how often you cut.
- **Short length.** The two shortest videos (13–14s) are both flops. Being short doesn't save a short
  with no story.
- **Loudness.** The flops range from −7.9 to −19.8 LUFS. It's a quality-control issue, not the cause.
- **Split screen alone.** China used a split (robots on top, Kai below) and flopped. A split works when
  the halves are **BTS + result of the same action**.

**Does separate (winners 4/4 vs flops 0/3):**
1. **A concrete stake in the first line or the title**: 1 take, Netflix "first ever", $800,000, $2.5M
   venue. The flops have an abstract question, nothing, or "impossible" with no proof.
2. **Kai's craft is visible early.** The pilot/rig/builder is *doing the thing* at 0.0–3.0s in 3 of 4
   winners. In the flops, craft appears at 7.1s for 1s (China), never (2-cam), or for <1s before a cut
   (Avata).
3. **A visual payoff the viewer was promised.** Countdown → launch → reveal, problem → permit → crowd
   shot. The flops end with no payoff (China), no verdict (2-cam), or a payoff that only looks hard to
   pilots (Avata).
4. **The title names the stake or a brand.** "1 TAKE", "Netflix's 1st Music Festival", "@ludwig". The
   flops use a mood ("Idk what's happening anymore") or model numbers.

**Confound to rule out:** all 3 flops were posted **2026-10-15 → 2026-11-06**; every winner is from
2023–2025. A channel-wide drop in Shorts distribution would look the same. **Next step:** pull Kai's
YouTube Studio numbers for these 3 (impressions, "viewed vs swiped away" %, average % viewed) and for
anything else posted since October, to separate content from channel effects.

## Diagnosis per flop

### China recap (`kai_yt_zfKBLxp6I_g`): 1,456 views
**One line:** an abstract AI-anxiety question over someone else's robot footage gave Kai's FPV audience
no stake, no craft and no payoff to wait for, and 76s of personal reflection is long-form content in a
Shorts slot.
- **0.0–5.7s:** "is there even a point of creating anymore?" No number, person at risk or promise. The
  winners' first lines are a challenge (1 take) or a brag (Netflix, $800K).
- **Frame 1:** robots + desk talking head. FPV gear appears **once, at 7.1s, for ~1s**. Winners show
  the pilot at 0.0s.
- **China isn't mentioned until 10.3s** and isn't in the title. The title ("Idk what's happening
  anymore") has no searchable topic.
- **75.8s** with no tension device. The only other 75s+ reference (the #1, 79s) is held up by a
  one-take.
- **No visual payoff:** it ends on "reason enough to keep going" (73s) over a workshop shot.
- 220 wpm VO with no pauses or reaction beats. **−19.8 LUFS**, the quietest reference.
- *(Guess)* Its 3.0% like rate is healthy, so people who *did* watch liked it. The problem is
  distribution: the first 2–3s didn't hold cold viewers, so it wasn't pushed. Kai likes it because the
  story matters to *him*. It belongs in long form or as a community/IG carousel, or recut around one
  concrete moment.

### 2-camera setup vs result (`kai_yt_L9RJX-rQ0_E`): 6,615 views
**One line:** a letterboxed product shot with no person, no voice and no verdict asks viewers to spot a
difference that isn't visible on a phone.
- **0.0–2.25s:** a 16:9 clip letterboxed inside 9:16, with **~half the screen black**.
- **No Kai at any point.** Effectively no speech (one word at 1.84s).
- **2.25–14.0s:** two near-identical stacked results. No verdict, winner label or stress test.
- Tiny product labels; the title is two model numbers. 0.77% like rate, the lowest of Kai's set.

### Avata 360 iPhone BTS vs final (`kai_yt_LDtUDGfn4KE`): 3,527 views
**One line:** the BTS and result play one after the other instead of together, and nothing on screen
says why the shot is "impossible", so non-pilots see a guy in a parking lot and then a nice chase shot.
- **0.0–2.5s:** a mundane wide shot of a kart; the drone is barely visible. "Woo-hoo, let's go!"
  carries no information.
- **Kai appears at 2.5s and is cut away at 3.3s**, after which the BTS never returns. The winners keep
  BTS on screen for the **whole** runtime (wQsN from 0.0s, #1 from 3s).
- **No on-screen text at all.** "Impossible" lives only in the title, and the result (a tilted chase)
  doesn't *look* impossible to non-pilots.
- 13.2s, no payoff line.

## Landon as outside benchmark (craft, not reach)
Landon's 58M views come with a **0.23% like rate** on a sponsored post, so they're probably partly paid
*(guess)*. What's worth copying:
- **Frame 1 = an incongruity** (a 6-year-old at a pro edit bay).
- **The setup sentence carries its own danger** ("…and then I left for a week"), with explicit stakes by 7.5s.
- **Every noun gets a shot** (plane on "left"), at 6.7 cuts/10s.
- **Named characters** (Theo, Scout, Simon) as re-hooks every ~8–10s, and a loop re-opened at 21s
  ("not the only one").
- **One-word captions**, large and centred. Loudness on target (−13.2).

Kai's winners already do the first two (countdown/1-take, "for Netflix's first ever"). The flops do none of them.

## Testable rules
1. **Craft in frame 1.** The pilot, rig or builder doing the thing, at readable size, by 0.0s and
   no later than 3.0s. *Test:* the next BTS short opens on Kai in goggles; compare the 3s hold rate vs
   the Avata short.
2. **A concrete stake in the first sentence AND the title**: a number, a brand, "1 take", a deadline.
   Never an abstract question or a mood title.
3. **BTS format = simultaneous split for the whole runtime**, never BTS-then-result. *Test:* recut the
   Avata 360 short as a split (iPhone on top, result below) and repost.
4. **Promise a visual payoff in the first 3s and deliver it in the last 3s.** No footage-only tail
   longer than 3s after the last line.
5. **Storytime needs 2+ obstacles or failures before the payoff** (Netflix: 3 in 8s; Ludwig: 2).
6. **Comparisons need an on-screen verdict and a difference visible on a phone** (stress-test it) or
   they don't ship as Shorts.
7. **Never letterbox. Fill 9:16** from frame 1.
8. **Reflective or recap pieces go to long form or IG carousel**, unless recut around one concrete,
   stakes-driven moment that opens the short.
9. **Sound-off check:** frame 1 + on-screen text must say what's happening and why it matters without
   audio (the Avata short had no text at all).
10. **Export QC at −14 ±1 LUFS** (China −19.8, Ludwig −18.8). It's not the cause of the flops, but
    it's a free fix.
